Instruments

Trade instruments explained

Letter of Credit

A document issued by a bank to its client (importer or exporter), indicating that the issuing bank will honour and pay a specified amount on demand when specified documents are presented. It usually sets payment conditions such as amount, currency and expiry date. It is a universally recognised instrument in international trade, most frequently used in importing transactions.

Standby LC

An SBLC is a bank guarantee in Letter of Credit format. As banks in the US were not permitted to issue guarantees, US banks adapted the guarantee format to a Letter of Credit as an alternative.

Letter of Commitment

Commitment letters are contracts obtained by borrowers to assure the availability of financing from a lead lender.

Documentary Collection

The collection of a sum of money due from a buyer to a bank against delivery of shipping documents.

Factoring and Invoice Discounting

In factoring, the supplier sells receivables in the form of invoices to the factor, who advances 60 to 85% of the receivable amount. Invoice discounting lets businesses use the value of goods purchased as collateral, giving them enough cash flow for the period before their buyer pays, while maintaining a profit margin.
